POCKET MONSTERS: MINAMOTO

Arena Ecology System

Combat Systems Design Reference  ·  v2.1

Internal design document. Full-system revision incorporating: expanded 22-category move taxonomy, 10-habitat + 7-characteristic Pokémon taxonomy, corrected critical hit formula, revised Jiro/Brock/Misty/Surge arenas, and type-advantage mitigation philosophy. Sabrina excluded pending character confirmation.

**Part 0 — Global Rules**

**0.1  Gym Leader Focus Sash**

Every Pokémon on a Gym Leader's team carries an implicit Focus Sash effect. If a move would reduce the Pokémon from above 0 HP to 0 HP in a single hit, the Pokémon instead survives at 1 HP. This triggers once per Pokémon per battle. The purpose is structural: every Gym Leader Pokémon is guaranteed at least one action before being knocked out, preventing sweeping before the arena mechanics can express themselves. This rule is universal — it applies to all Gym Leaders, the Four Heavenly Kings, and Boss encounters.

**0.2  Type Advantage Mitigation Philosophy**

Gym Leaders are monotype specialists. A specialist's advantage is not raw type strength — it is mastery of their own environment. Each arena is designed so that the structurally correct type is still effective, but the environment creates a new cost or liability around using it. The goal is not to nerf type advantage but to make the arena itself a third combatant that the correct type must navigate alongside the Gym Leader's team.

Practical examples: Ground-type against Surge is immune to everything Surge threatens, but Dig is disabled and Surge carries Flying coverage. Water-type against Misty is the correct TIDE tool but each Aquatic use pushes toward higher Conductivity that can detonate both sides. Fire-type against Misty is not effective damage but creates MIST that suspends Misty's TIDE advantage — a utility role that makes Fire genuinely relevant without being type-correct.

Every gym design should be audited against this principle: what is the arena doing to make the correct type answer expensive, and is that cost interesting rather than arbitrary?

**0.3  Modified Critical Hit Formula**

All battles use the following crit stage table. Stages +3 and +4 are increased above standard.

| **Stage** | **Probability** | **Common Pathways** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +0 | 6.25% | Base rate. No modifiers. |
| +1 | 12.5% | High-crit moves (Slash, Leek Slap, Drill Peck, Razor Leaf, Dive Bomb). |
| +2 | 25% | Focus Energy use. Leek item on Farfetch'd. Arena state bonus (first-mover in ACTIVE). |
| +3 | 50% | Focus Energy + high-crit move. Arena state bonus (first-mover in FULL). |
| +4 | 100% | Farfetch'd holding Leek + FULL AIRFLOW + first-mover. No other standard path reaches this. |

**⚑ **Farfetch'd + Leek: the item grants +2 crit stages. The arena's first-mover bonus in FULL grants +2 stages. Combined = +4 = 100%. Neither source alone reaches +4. Farfetch'd must be in FULL AIRFLOW AND act before the opponent in that turn to land a guaranteed crit. This is the fight's signature moment — earned, not given.

**Part 1 — Move Category Taxonomy**

Moves are assigned to ecology categories based on their physical mechanism, not their type. A category describes what the move does to the environment, not what damage it deals. Moves may belong to multiple categories (e.g. Fire Punch = THERMAL-HOT + CONTACT; Wing Attack = WIND + CONTACT-BLADE). Arena rules always reference categories, never individual move names.

The existing GDD subcategory field (BODY, ARMS, FISTS, KICKS, TEETH, CLAWS, HORNS, TAIL) classifies the body part used within the CONTACT category and remains unchanged.

**Part 2 — Pokémon Taxonomy**

**2.1  Habitat (Mutually Exclusive)**

Every Pokémon belongs to exactly one primary habitat. This classification is readable from PokéDex entries and reflects the natural environment from which the species originates. Habitat determines a Pokémon's ecological baseline — how it navigates the world, not just where it lives.

| **Habitat** | **Ecological Profile** | **Representative Species** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grasslands | Open prairie, farmland, and low-altitude plains. Adapted to wide visibility, low cover, and variable weather. | Rattata/Rattatak, Ekans/Arbo, Nidoran lines, Mankey/Okorizaru, Kentauros, Meowth/Persian (Nyarth/Nyako) |
| Water's Edge | Rivers, lakeshores, and coastal wetlands. Amphibious or shore-dependent species. | Psyduck/Golduck (Koduck/Zuduck), Slowpoke/Slowbro (Slowmo), Poliwag line, Seel/Dewgong, Horsea/Seadra (Tattu/Seadra) |

**2.2  Characteristics (Overlapping)**

A Pokémon may have one or more characteristics that describe how it moves and interacts with its environment. Characteristics overlap — a Pokémon can be both Aerial and Liminal (e.g., Haunter). Habitat and Characteristic are independent axes: a Mountain habitat Pokémon that can fly (Aerodactyl) has both Mountain habitat and Aerial characteristic.

| **Characteristic** | **Definition** | **Primary Arena Relevance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aerial | Moves primarily through flight or levitation. Not reliant on surface contact. | Immune to ground-surface effects (terrain SPE penalties, Muddy). Reduced Sandstorm damage immunity (NOT standard Rock/Ground immunity — see 0.3). Exposed to altitude Conductive bonus in Surge's arena. |
| Swimmer | Primary locomotion is aquatic. Can operate normally in water environments. | Immune to wet-terrain SPE/EVA penalties in Brock's arena. No ACC penalty on Misty's platform. Aquatic moves available natively. |
| Terrain-dweller | Adapted to rocky, unstable, or elevated terrain. | Immune to TERRAIN-tier SPE/EVA penalties (Brock). Mountain habitat and Terrain-dweller characteristic are strongly correlated but not identical. Rock/Ground/Steel types are the clearest examples. |
| Ground-bound | Default. Primary locomotion on flat terrain surface. | Full exposure to all terrain, atmospheric, and toxin states. Most of any player's early-game team. |
| Subterranean | Adapted to underground or lightless spaces. | Native users of EXCAVATING moves. In arenas where Dig is disabled (Surge), this characteristic provides no bypass — the physical impossibility of penetrating the surface applies regardless of adaptation. |
| Liminal | Connected to the boundary between life and death through species lore or origin. | Immune to TP drain effects in Erika's arena. Attacks against Liminal targets do not escalate SPIRIT PRESSURE. Liminal category determined by species (see below) — it is not a type classification. Ghost-type ≠ automatically Liminal (some Ghost-types may not qualify). |
| Volatile | Currently in an elevated off-ground state (using Fly, Bounce, or natural levitation in this turn). | Immune to discharge blast in Surge's arena (elevated above ground arc). CONDUCTIVE moves still hit Volatile at normal power. Volatile is a battle-state characteristic, not a permanent species trait. |

The following species qualify as Liminal for arena ecology purposes. This list is design-authoritative — it is derived from species lore, not from type or stats.

**GYM 1  ·  Flying Type**

**JIRO**

LUCKY STRIKE

**Competitive Theme: Turn Order and Critical Hits**

**1.1  Core Environmental Model**

**State Variable: **AIRFLOW — STILL / ACTIVE / FULL (linear; transitions via move categories)

**Overlay A: **SANDSTORM — ABSENT / PRESENT (3 turns; independent expiry; triggered by SAND ATTACK in ACTIVE or FULL)

**Overlay B: **HUMIDITY — ABSENT / PRESENT (3 turns; triggered by AQUATIC moves; reduces sandstorm chip)

| **State** | **AIRFLOW** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STILL | 0 | All ventilation columns blocked or inactive. No current. Aerial Pokémon penalized. |
| ACTIVE | 1 | Columns running. Moderate current. Aerial advantage begins. |
| FULL | 2 (max) | Maximum output. Peak aerial dominance. The arena at full operational capacity. |

**Transition Rules**

- WIND category moves → +1 AIRFLOW (STILL→ACTIVE, ACTIVE→FULL). Capped at FULL.

- SUPPRESSION category moves (String Shot, Smokescreen, Squid Ink, etc.) → immediate −1 AIRFLOW per use. Sticky or obstructing materials immediately clog the vent columns.

- SAND ATTACK (SUPPRESSION) in ACTIVE or FULL → activates SANDSTORM overlay for 3 turns. In STILL, Sand Attack only reduces accuracy normally.

- AQUATIC moves → activates HUMIDITY overlay for 3 turns. Does NOT suppress SANDSTORM. Both overlays can coexist.

- SANDSTORM expires naturally after 3 turns. No move accelerates or extends it after triggering.

- HUMIDITY expires naturally after 3 turns.

**1.2  System Rules**

| **State / Overlay** | **Effect on Aerial / Volatile Pokémon** | **Effect on Ground-bound Pokémon** | **First-Mover Effect** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| STILL | −1 SPE (updraft collapse; they relied on the current). | No modifier. | No crit bonus. |
| ACTIVE | +1 SPE. | No modifier. | +1 Crit Stage. |
| FULL | +1 SPE. | −1 EVA (pressure differential disorients low-altitude movement). | +2 Crit Stages. |
| + SANDSTORM | 1/8 HP damage end of turn. NOT immune to sandstorm damage (non-Ground, non-Rock type). | 1/8 HP damage end of turn. −1 EVA stacks in FULL. | Crit bonus from base AIRFLOW state still applies. |
| + HUMIDITY | Sandstorm damage reduced to 1/16 (moisture dampens particulate). SPE modifier unchanged. | Sandstorm damage reduced to 1/16. | No crit interaction. |
| THERMAL-HOT in ACTIVE/FULL | No direct effect. | No direct effect. | Enclosed warehouse retains heat: fire moves gain +10% power for 2 turns after any THERMAL-HOT use in ACTIVE or FULL. Both sides benefit equally. |

**⚑ **Sandstorm affects ALL non-Mountain, non-Ground-type Pokémon — including Flying/Volatile. Jiro's own Aerial team is damaged by sandstorm equally. This is physically consistent: airborne Pokémon are exposed to the full particulate concentration with no terrain cover.

**Species-Level Hooks**

- Farfetch'd (Kamonegi) + Leek: item grants +2 crit stages. In FULL as first-mover: +2 additional stages = +4 = 100% crit. This is the fight's signature scenario. Leek Slap (HIGH CRIT CHANCE + CONTACT) is naturally a high-crit move (+1 base), Leek pushes to +3 normally and +4 in FULL first-mover. Farfetch'd is meaningfully faster in FULL — the player must suppress AIRFLOW to prevent Jiro reaching this state.

- Pidgeot (Pigeot): Hurricane (WIND+Confusion) is the highest-power single AIRFLOW pusher. If Pidgeot gets a Hurricane turn while already in FULL, no additional AIRFLOW gain but Confusion effect applies. In ACTIVE, Hurricane pushes to FULL and applies Confusion simultaneously — two-function turn.

- Geodude line (Mountain): Immune to EVA penalty and sandstorm. Rock Throw (PROJECTILE, −10% ACC in FULL) is accuracy-penalized. Rock Slide (GROUND-IMPACT) is not. Geodude should not rely on PROJECTILE in FULL.

**1.5  Strategic Layer**

**Core Decision Each Turn**

The player is choosing between: using a SUPPRESSION move (−1 AIRFLOW, costs an offensive turn) vs. attacking into a progressively Jiro-favored environment. Jiro responds to each SUPPRESSION with WIND — so the player needs two consecutive SUPPRESSION actions to drop AIRFLOW one level, costing two offensive turns against Jiro's one.

**Mountain Shortcut**

Mountain-archetype Pokémon (Geodude line) ignore FULL EVA, sandstorm damage, and AIRFLOW effects entirely. They do not need to manage the arena — they simply fight. This is the lesson: knowing your team's ecological register is more efficient than managing the field with a mismatched team.

**Thermal-Hot Window**

The room temperature bonus (+10% fire power for 2 turns) is symmetric — both players benefit. If the player has a Fire-type, using Ember to establish the window and following with Flamethrower is a small but real 2-turn offensive boost. Jiro will have Fire coverage on at least one Pokémon for exactly this reason.

**BROCK**

**Competitive Theme: Speed Control and Terrain Restraint**

**2.1  Core Environmental Model**

**Field Constant: **All non-Terrain-dweller Pokémon begin with −1 SPE. Permanent; active in all terrain states.

**State Variable 1: **TERRAIN — INTACT / CRACKED / FRACTURED (one-directional progression)

**State Variable 2: **MOISTURE — DRY / MUDDY (toggleable; MUDDY has no natural decay — requires WIND to revert)

**Overlay: **SANDSTORM — ABSENT / PRESENT (2 turns; triggered by WIND in DRY state)

- WIND category in MUDDY → MUDDY → DRY (dries terrain). Does not trigger SANDSTORM (damp terrain can't blow up sand).

**⚑ **TERRAIN tier is permanent within the battle — cannot be restored once fractured. MOISTURE is the only reversible variable. The battlefield deteriorates over the course of the fight.

- If player uses BLAST: immediately reaches FRACTURED, often harming the player's own non-Terrain-dweller team before Brock needs to.

- Terrain-dweller (Mountain habitat Pokémon) bypass all of this. They fight normally while the arena collapses around everyone else. Brock's team is already adapted. The player's team needs to be too.

- If player uses AQUATIC → MUDDY: Brock switches to non-Ground-Impact attacks and waits. He does not benefit from MUDDY. He waits for WIND or natural transition back to DRY.

- Against Terrain-dweller opponents: recognizes structural immunity. Switches to non-Ground-Impact coverage moves. Does not invest in terrain deterioration that won't affect this opponent.

**GYM 3  ·  Water Type**

**MISTY**

LITTLE MERMAID

**Competitive Theme: Momentum and Accuracy Disruption**

**3.1  Core Environmental Model**

**Field Constant: **CONDUCTIVE BUOYS — Electric moves always hit regardless of accuracy modifiers. Power reduced to 50% (buoys absorb and redirect current). All non-Aquatic Pokémon −1 ACC baseline.

**State Variable 1: **TIDE — NEUTRAL / PLAYER / OPPONENT (shifts with last AQUATIC use)

**State Variable 2: **CONDITION — CLEAR / MIST / FROZEN

| **State** | **TIDE** | **CONDITION** | **Active Effects** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NEUTRAL-CLEAR | Neither | Clear | Baseline only: −1 ACC non-Aquatic. |

**Transition Rules**

- AQUATIC moves in CLEAR → TIDE shifts to user immediately.

- AQUATIC moves in MIST → TIDE does not shift (suspended). Damage applies normally.

- AQUATIC moves in FROZEN → TIDE does not shift (locked). Damage applies normally.

- THERMAL-HOT moves → CONDITION becomes MIST (3 turns). Overrides FROZEN. TIDE ownership freezes at current holder during MIST.

- THERMAL-COLD moves when MIST → clears MIST immediately, then CONDITION → FROZEN (2 turns).

- THERMAL-COLD moves in CLEAR or FROZEN → CONDITION → FROZEN (2 turns; resets duration if already FROZEN).

**⚑ **Fire moves are type-neutral vs Water but have genuine strategic utility here: they create MIST, which suspends Misty's TIDE advantage and applies −1 ACC to all Pokémon including Aquatic. Fire is not a damage solution — it is a field disruption tool. This is the type-advantage mitigation principle in action.

**3.2  System Rules**

| **Mechanic** | **Description** |

| MIST ACC penalty | All Pokémon −1 ACC (including Aquatic). Stacks with the baseline non-Aquatic −1 ACC. Non-Aquatic in MIST: −2 total. Aquatic in MIST: −1 (first time they have any accuracy penalty in this arena). |

| FROZEN + TIDE lock | TIDE ownership is locked for 2 turns. The locked owner keeps the +50% Aquatic power bonus. The opponent cannot steal TIDE even with Aquatic moves. MIST (Thermal-Hot) overrides and cancels FROZEN. |

TIDE is the meta-game. Every AQUATIC move is simultaneously damage and territorial claim. The optimal play is gain TIDE → THERMAL-COLD → FROZEN → attack at +50% power for 2 turns. Misty breaks this with THERMAL-HOT (MIST overrides FROZEN). The fight is about who sequences their CONDITION transitions better.

- When she owns TIDE in CLEAR: uses THERMAL-COLD to FREEZE immediately (locks advantage).

**GYM 4  ·  Electric Type**

**SURGE**

GENERAL ELECTRIC

**Competitive Theme: Risk Management and Punish Windows**

**4.1  Core Environmental Model**

**Field Object: **TESLA COIL — shared field object with its own discrete state. One coil serves both sides.

**State Variable: **COIL STATE — ACTIVE(0) / ACTIVE(1) / ACTIVE(2) / DISCHARGING(3) / SHORTED / DESTROYED

**Field Constraint: **Metal container enclosure: EXCAVATING moves (Dig, Tunneling) are disabled — the floor cannot be penetrated. Sand Attack disabled (no sand material). Earthquake produces RESONANCE effect instead of standard ground-penetration.

| **COIL State** | **Description** | **How Reached** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ACTIVE 0 | Coil functional. No charge. No threat. | Battle start. After SHORTED expires. After DISCHARGING resets. |
| ACTIVE 1–2 | Charge building. Discharge risk accumulates. | Conductive moves resolving without KO: +1 CHARGE. |
| DISCHARGING (3) | Discharge fires this turn. Charge resets to 0 after. | CHARGE accumulates to 3. |
| SHORTED | Coil disabled by water infiltration. 2 turns duration. | Any AQUATIC move → immediate SHORT. CHARGE resets to 0. Cannot accumulate during SHORT. Reactivates at ACTIVE 0 after 2 turns. |
| DESTROYED | Coil permanently disabled. No more discharge for this battle. | 3 cumulative THERMAL-HOT moves used → OVERHEAT counter reaches 3 → permanent destruction. |

**Transition Rules**

- CONDUCTIVE moves resolving without KO → +1 CHARGE. KO produces no charge (energy fully discharged on impact, nothing retained in the system).

- AQUATIC moves → COIL immediately SHORTED for 2 turns. CHARGE resets to 0.

- THERMAL-HOT moves → OVERHEAT counter +1. At 3 cumulative: DESTROYED. Permanent.

- GROUND-IMPACT moves (except disabled Dig) → RESONANCE: −1 CHARGE. Earthquake vibration grounds the accumulated charge through the metal structure. Earthquake deals standard damage AND reduces CHARGE simultaneously.

- SONIC moves (Screech, Supersonic, Sonicboom) → RESONANCE partial: −0.5 CHARGE (rounds down, minimum −0 per turn, but contributes over two uses). Vibration frequency partially grounds the coil. Minor interaction, not a primary strategy.

**⚑ **DESTROYED is irreversible. It requires 3 turns of off-type (non-Electric) THERMAL-HOT moves. This is a deliberate strategic commitment — 3 turns of suboptimal fire damage to permanently remove the coil threat. Surge has no ability to prevent this once the player commits.

**4.2  System Rules**

| **COIL State** | **Effect** |
| --- | --- |
| ACTIVE 0–2 | Standard field. No discharge hazard. Conductive moves deal normal damage with normal paralysis secondary chance. |
| DISCHARGING (3) | Fixed damage to all non-Electric, non-Volatile Pokémon on both sides simultaneously. Damage value predetermined (based on Surge's highest ATK Pokémon stat). All survivors paralyzed for 2 turns. Paralyzed Pokémon take +30% damage from all sources. CHARGE resets to 0 after discharge. Volatile Pokémon (Fly state, levitating) are elevated above the ground arc — completely immune to discharge damage and paralysis. |
| SHORTED (2 turns) | No CHARGE accumulation possible. No discharge risk. Conductive moves function normally as single-target attacks (coil offline, no system to accumulate into). Coil reactivates at ACTIVE 0 on turn 3. |
| DESTROYED (permanent) | No CHARGE system. No discharge ever. Surge fights as a standard trainer. The arena's primary field mechanic is gone. |

Metal container effects: Earthquake in a metal room creates RESONANCE (−1 CHARGE + standard damage). Dig and Tunneling are physically impossible (metal floor). Sand Attack produces nothing (no sand). The arena partially neutralizes the Ground-type toolset — structurally correct type, but one key tool disabled and the remaining tools (Earthquake) redirected through the RESONANCE mechanic.

**4.3  Interaction Matrix — Move Categories**

| **Category** | **Effect on COIL** | **Modified by COIL State** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CONDUCTIVE | +1 CHARGE if no KO. 0 CHARGE on KO. | Standard single-target in all COIL states. Electric-type Pokémon immune to discharge and to the discharge paralysis. Volatile immune to discharge burst. Conductive is the primary charge source for both sides — KO'ing with Conductive is always the clean resolution. |
| AQUATIC | SHORTED for 2 turns. CHARGE → 0. | Type-effective vs Electric. PRIMARY coil management tool. SHORT is temporary — 2 turns without accumulation, then reactivates. The player who uses AQUATIC every 2 turns maintains SHORT but sacrifices offensive output. Surge has anti-Water coverage to contest this. |

No other move category offers permanent DESTROYED. Surge cannot prevent it — he can only deny the player 3 turns of Fire attacks through faster discharges. |
| GROUND-IMPACT | −1 CHARGE (RESONANCE). Earthquake also deals standard damage. | Earthquake is dual-function: damages Electric-type Pokémon AND reduces CHARGE. Ground-type Pokémon immune to discharge and CONDUCTIVE. The structural counter — but EXCAVATING moves are disabled. Surge has Flying-type coverage specifically for Ground. |

Screech and Supersonic are standard elsewhere; here they have a trace-level CHARGE management effect. **4.4  Strategic Layer**

**Three Paths to COIL Management**

SHORT (AQUATIC): efficient type-correct, temporary. Surge rebuilds at ACTIVE 0 after 2 turns. Ongoing water use is maintenance, not resolution.

DESTROYED (THERMAL-HOT ×3): permanent but 3 off-type turns. Once DESTROYED, the fight normalizes. The investment pays out for the rest of the battle.

RESONANCE (GROUND-IMPACT / Earthquake): dual-function — reduces CHARGE AND deals damage. But Surge has Flying coverage for Ground-types, and Dig is disabled. Earthquake is the best single-turn option but Surge anticipates it.

**Discharge as Designed Threat**

Surge manages CHARGE deliberately — stops Conductive at CHARGE 2, switches to Thunder Wave (paralysis without CHARGE accumulation) to set up the +30% damage multiplier before completing the discharge. He is not trying to discharge himself — he is engineering a state where discharge benefits only him.

| Fire | Off-type. DESTROYED at 3 uses — permanent coil removal. | None after DESTROYED. The risk is the 3 turns of off-type fire damage on the path to DESTROYED. Surge plays fast and will try to discharge before the 3rd THERMAL-HOT lands. |

**4.6  AI Utilization Model**

- Surge uses Conductive up to CHARGE 2, then stops — switches to Thunder Wave, Leer, or non-Conductive attacks. The paralysis setup turn maximizes the +30% bonus once discharge fires from the player's accumulation.

- When player AQUATICS to SHORT: Surge uses coverage or non-Conductive attacks for 2 turns (SHORT is offline anyway), resumes Conductive the moment SHORT expires.

- He never uses THERMAL-HOT (would burn his own OVERHEAT counter toward DESTROYED).

- He targets Ground-type opponents with Flying-type coverage — he has anticipated the structural counter.

- Optimal Surge sequence: CHARGE 2 → Thunder Wave → CHARGE 3 via player's Conductive → discharge + paralysis multiplier active → high-power follow-up.

| THERMAL-HOT against Liminal | +1 (damaging move). | No PRESSURE bonus beyond standard +1. Fire has no special spiritual connection. Does not advance PRESSURE faster than any other damaging category. |

**5.3  Strategic Layer**

The composition decision is made before the battle. Liminal Pokémon bypass TP drain entirely and attack without PRESSURE escalation. Non-Liminal Pokémon with access to Dark-type moves (effective vs Ghost, no type-level Spectral resonance) are the secondary answer. HAZE as a carried move gives any Pokémon access to −2 PRESSURE.

A player who enters with a non-Liminal Contact-heavy physical team will Struggle within 6–8 turns at PRESSURE 3–4. The arena teaches the lesson through exhaustion rather than through sudden KO — which is more pedagogically honest.

**5.4  AI Utilization Model**

- Erika uses damaging Spectral moves every turn (Liminal Pokémon, +0 PRESSURE from her actions).

- She uses HAZE only when PRESSURE unexpectedly benefits the player (e.g., player is using Liminal Pokémon that are gaining from PRESSURE escalation somehow — rare edge case).

- Against Liminal player teams: she fights as a standard type matchup. No TP system applies to her side. She switches to coverage moves if the PRESSURE system is neutralized.

**GYM 6  ·  Poison Type**

**KOGA**

PURPLE HAZE

**Competitive Theme: Attrition Management and Active Rotation**

**6.1  Core Environmental Model**

**Field Constant: **All non-Poison Pokémon are immediately poisoned upon entering the arena. Unconditional. The question is how fast the poison acts, not whether it acts.

**State Variable: **MIASMA DENSITY — 0 (THIN) / 1 (LOW) / 2 (THICK) / 3 (DENSE)

| **Archetype / Layer \ DENSITY** | **THIN (0)** | **LOW (1)** | **THICK (2)** | **DENSE (3)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aerial (High layer) | 1/32/turn | 1/32/turn | 1/16/turn | 1/16/turn |
| Mountain (Mid layer) | 1/16/turn | 1/16/turn | 1/8/turn | 1/8/turn |
| Ground-bound / Cave / Swimmer (Floor) | 1/16/turn | 1/8/turn | 1/8/turn | 1/4/turn |
| Poison-native (any layer) | Immune | Immune | Immune | Immune |

**Transition Rules**

- TOXIC category moves → +1 DENSITY.

- POWDER/SPORE category moves (Sleep Powder, Spore, Moth Scales, Spike Barrage) → +1 DENSITY.

- SMOKESCREEN (SUPPRESSION) → +1 DENSITY (occlusive particles amplify miasma).

- BLAST category (Self-Destruct, Explosion) → +2 DENSITY immediately.

- WIND category → −2 DENSITY per use. Primary active management tool.

- DISPLACEMENT category → −1 DENSITY per use. Partial dispersal.

- Rain Dance (weather SUPPORT) → −1 DENSITY (rainfall washes particulate down).

- HAZE → DENSITY resets to 0 immediately.

**6.2  Additional Mechanical Rules**

| **Trigger** | **Effect** |
| --- | --- |
| ACID (TOXIC category) | Standard toxic damage + drops target one layer (permanently for this switch-in). A Floor Pokémon hit by Acid: if already at Floor, poisons at current DENSITY rate instantly (accelerated). Koga's most efficient double-function move. |
| Contact moves by Aerial Pokémon | To reach a ground-level opponent, Aerial must descend through the miasma. On the turn a Contact move is used: Aerial takes the Floor layer poison rate for that turn, not their Aerial rate. Returns to Aerial rate next turn. Repeated Contact builds meaningful accumulated exposure. |
| AGILITY used by non-Poison | SPE and EVA increase apply normally. Additional: increased movement speed = faster breathing = 1.5× current DENSITY poison rate for 2 turns post-use. The cost of speed in a toxic atmosphere. |
| REST by non-Poison | During sleep: reduced biological activity = poison rate halved for sleep duration. Awakening resumes full DENSITY rate. REST provides a brief metabolic slowdown, not a cure. |
| Antidote / Heal from Bag | Clears current poison status. Pokémon is immediately re-poisoned (still breathing miasma). Buys one clean turn before poison resumes at current DENSITY rate. Strategic delay tool — not a solution. |
| Poison-native vs Koga coverage | Koga explicitly runs Psychic-type and Ground-type coverage for Poison-immune compositions. Structural immunity is anticipated. |

**6.3  Key Move Interactions**

| **Category** | **Effect on DENSITY** | **Specific Examples** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TOXIC | +1 DENSITY. ACID additionally drops target layer. | Toxic, Acid, Venom Shot, Sludge, Poison Gas, Gastro Acid — all accumulate DENSITY. Koga chains these deliberately. |
| POWDER/SPORE | +1 DENSITY. | Spore (signature of Parasyte — 100% sleep + DENSITY), Moth Scales (PARALYSIS/POISON/SLEEP + DENSITY), Sleep Powder, Stun Spore. Powder/Spore in this arena serves double duty: status AND DENSITY escalation. |
| WIND | −2 DENSITY. | Gust, Whirlwind, Hurricane. Aerial Pokémon natively have Wind moves — they are simultaneously the most survivable archetype AND the most capable DENSITY managers. Pidgeot's Hurricane is the single best per-turn DENSITY management move available to the player. |
| BLAST | +2 DENSITY. | Self-Destruct, Explosion. A player Pokémon using Explosion in this arena accelerates DENSITY for their remaining team. Double-penalty: explosion damage AND DENSITY spike. |
| DISPLACEMENT | −1 DENSITY. | Whirlwind (also WIND: −2 DENSITY total when both apply), Roar. Partial dispersal. Less efficient than WIND alone but available on more Pokémon. |
| HAZE | Resets DENSITY to 0. | Complete reset. Koga never uses HAZE himself. Player HAZE use is countered by Koga immediately using a TOXIC move to begin re-accumulation. |

**6.4  Strategic Layer**

Rotation timing is the core skill. The player's non-Poison Pokémon are on a timer determined by their archetype and the current DENSITY. The optimal rhythm: use a Ground-bound Pokémon for 1–2 turns, switch before DENSE kills it, let the switched-out Pokémon recover HP with bag items, bring it back when DENSITY has been partially reduced by a Wind user. Aerial Pokémon with Wind moves are the linchpin: they survive longest and actively reduce the field state.

**6.5  Type Interaction Reframing**

| **Type** | **Why Still Effective** | **Arena Cost** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poison | Complete DENSITY immunity. Full field freedom. | Koga has explicit Psychic and Ground coverage. Type immunity to miasma does not mean immunity to Koga's team. |
| Flying | Aerial characteristic: best DENSITY survival. Wind moves available natively. | Contact-reliant Flying moves require descent to Floor layer on use turn. At DENSE: Floor layer = 1/4 HP that turn. Projectile Flying moves avoid this entirely. |
| Ground | Immune to Poison-type moves (type immunity). | Ground characteristic = Floor layer exposure. Type immunity to Poison-type moves does NOT confer miasma immunity. Ground types breathe the same air. This is the key asymmetry: Ground types take 1/4 HP/turn from miasma at DENSE while being immune to Koga's Toxic moves. |
| Psychic | Super-effective vs Poison. No DENSITY interaction from Psychic-Wave moves. | Ground-bound characteristic if using Ground-bound Psychic-types. Maximum floor exposure. |

**6.6  AI Utilization Model**

- Opens with ACID on the first Ground-bound Pokémon (if present) — immediately poisons and drops layer before the player can respond.

- Chains TOXIC + POWDER/SPORE to reach DENSE (3) within 3–4 turns.

- Against Aerial Pokémon: waits for a Contact move (descent turn), applies Toxic that turn to poison at Floor rate before they return to Aerial layer.

- Against Poison-native teams: immediately switches to Psychic/Ground coverage. Does not waste TOXIC on immune targets.

- When player uses Antidote: uses TOXIC the very next turn to immediately re-apply poison.

- Never uses WIND or HAZE (both reduce his DENSITY investment).

**GYM 7  ·  Fire Type — DOUBLE BATTLE**

**COLEMAN**

FIRE IN THE HOLE

**Competitive Theme: Spread Attacks and Multi-Target Management**

**7.1  Core Environmental Model**

**Battle Format: **DOUBLE BATTLE. Coleman fields two Pokémon simultaneously. Player fields two Pokémon simultaneously.

**State Variable 1: **COMBUSTION — 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 (INFERNO)

**State Variable 2 / Overlay: **STEAM — ABSENT / PRESENT (3 turns; 4 turns after BLAST)

**Field Constant: **Entry burn: 1/16 HP on switch-in (every switch). Fire-native type: immune to entry burn. Rock/Steel archetype (Terrain-dweller): immune to entry burn. Aquatic characteristic: 2× entry burn (thermal shock). Aerial: immune to entry burn (no channel contact).

| **COMBUSTION** | **Fire Move Behavior** | **Ice Move Power** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–1 | No spread. Single-target. +25% Fire power baseline. | −25% (ambient heat degrades cold). |
| 2 | 25% chance to spread to one adjacent target at 50% power. | −25%. |
| 3 | Auto-spread to one adjacent target at 75% power. No roll. | Fails: 0%. |
| 4 (INFERNO) | All Fire moves hit all valid targets on both sides. Primary target: 100% power. All secondary targets: 50% power. | Fails: 0%. |

**Transition Rules**

- THERMAL-HOT category → +1 COMBUSTION per use. Capped at 4.

- AQUATIC category → −2 COMBUSTION AND activates STEAM for 3 turns.

- THERMAL-COLD → −1 COMBUSTION AND clears STEAM immediately.

- BLAST (Self-Destruct, Explosion) → +2 COMBUSTION AND activates STEAM for 4 turns simultaneously. The detonation releases stored heat across the forge.

- SUPPORT: HELPING HAND (Coleman's partner) targeting Coleman's attacker → +1 COMBUSTION immediately AND grants the next THERMAL-HOT move auto-spread regardless of actual COMBUSTION level. One-time override per HELPING HAND use. Coleman's most efficient two-turn escalation tool.

**7.2  System Rules**

| **State** | **Fire Power** | **Ice Power** | **All-Pokémon ACC** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COMBUSTION 0–1, DRY | +25% | −25% | Normal. |
| COMBUSTION 2–3, DRY | +25% (spread begins). | 0% at 3. | Normal. |
| COMBUSTION 4 (INFERNO), DRY | +50% | 0% | Normal. |
| ANY COMBUSTION + STEAM | +10% above current COMBUSTION baseline. | 0% at 2+. | −1 all (Fire-native immune to ACC penalty). |
| COMBUSTION 4 + STEAM | +60% total. | 0% | −2 all non-Fire-native. Worst-case state. |

**Double Battle Specific Rules**

- Earthquake and other GROUND-IMPACT spread moves in double battle: hit all non-Aerial on both sides. Player's own Ground-bound second Pokémon takes damage. Bringing an Aerial partner neutralizes self-damage.

- Explosion/Self-Destruct at COMBUSTION 3+: hits all targets both sides at full power (not halved). At COMBUSTION 4 + STEAM: EXPLOSION deals full power to all four slots simultaneously.

- Rock/Steel Terrain-dweller archetype: heat absorption — 2 full turns on field without switching grants +1 DEF. If struck by AQUATIC during the 2-turn window: +25% AQUATIC damage (thermal quench shock). Player must time Water hits outside the absorption window.

**7.3  Interaction Matrix — Move Categories**

| **Category** | **Effect on State** | **Modified by State** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| THERMAL-HOT | +1 COMBUSTION. | Power +25–50% depending on COMBUSTION. Each use improves next use's spread tier. Self-reinforcing escalation. Wildfire, Fireball, Flamethrower are primary COMBUSTION escalators. Flare Swirl (DOT+BURN) applies burn while escalating. |
| AQUATIC | −2 COMBUSTION + STEAM 3 turns. | Type-correct. Significantly reduces spread tier. But STEAM: +10% fire power AND −1 ACC all for 3 turns. Every Water use simultaneously reduces COMBUSTION and raises effective fire damage. Aquatic archetype: 2× entry burn, but their moves are the best COMBUSTION managers. |
| THERMAL-COLD | −1 COMBUSTION + clears STEAM. | Fails at 0% power at COMBUSTION 4 (INFERNO). At COMBUSTION 0–3: −25% power from ambient heat. Must reduce COMBUSTION with AQUATIC first before THERMAL-COLD has offensive value. Clears STEAM always regardless of damage. |
| BLAST | +2 COMBUSTION + STEAM 4 turns. | At COMBUSTION 3+: hits all 4 slots at full power. Followed by 4-turn STEAM at elevated COMBUSTION. Coleman uses BLAST as his endgame sequence when a Pokémon drops below 30% HP. |
| HELPING HAND (SUPPORT) | +1 COMBUSTION + auto-spread on next THERMAL-HOT. | Coleman's partner uses HELPING HAND → Coleman's attacker uses Wildfire/Flamethrower: guaranteed spread at any COMBUSTION level. Two-Pokémon coordination that makes the double battle format mechanically meaningful. |
| GROUND-IMPACT | No COMBUSTION effect. | Rock/Steel archetype: entry burn immune, DEF gain at 2 turns. Earthquake in double: hits all non-Aerial both sides. Player must pair Ground-Impact user with an Aerial partner to avoid self-damage. |

**7.4  Strategic Layer**

The double battle format means two decisions per turn. Managing COMBUSTION (via AQUATIC from one slot) while maintaining offensive pressure (from the other slot) is the core rhythm. Rock/Steel + Water is the optimal dual composition: Rock/Steel walls Fire and is entry-burn immune, Water manages COMBUSTION while accepting the STEAM trade.

EXPLOSION timing is the fight-defining threat. When Coleman's Pokémon drops below 30% HP: if STEAM is not active, his partner uses AQUATIC on the same turn (generating STEAM) and EXPLOSION follows the next turn. Player must track HP thresholds and reduce COMBUSTION below 3 before the threshold or ensure THERMAL-COLD was recently used to clear STEAM.

**7.5  Type Interaction Reframing**

| **Type** | **Why Still Effective** | **Arena Cost** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water | Type-effective. −2 COMBUSTION per use. Core spread-tier management. | 2× entry burn (Aquatic archetype). STEAM activates every use (+10% Fire power, −1 ACC all, 3 turns). Water is self-compounding — correct and costly simultaneously. |
| Rock/Ground | Entry burn immune (Rock/Steel archetype). Walls Fire damage. Earthquake: GROUND-IMPACT spread. | Earthquake hits player's own non-Aerial second Pokémon in double battle. Must pair with Aerial partner to neutralize self-hit. Heat absorption window: quench risk if Water hits during 2-turn window. |
| Ice | −1 COMBUSTION + clears STEAM. Strategic sequencing value. | 0% power at COMBUSTION 4. Requires prior AQUATIC use to reduce COMBUSTION below INFERNO. Ice is the second step in a two-step sequence, not an opener. |
| Fire mirror | Fire-native: entry burn immune, ACC penalty immune, STAB. | Each use: +1 COMBUSTION. Player's Fire moves accelerate Coleman's INFERNO. Correct archetype (immune to the arena), counterproductive state contribution. |

**7.6  AI Utilization Model**

- Turn 1: Partner HELPING HAND + Coleman THERMAL-HOT. +1 COMBUSTION from HELPING HAND, +1 from Thermal-Hot, auto-spread applied to the fire move. Immediate multi-target pressure from turn 1.

- Subsequent turns: Coleman Thermal-Hot every turn. Partner alternates between HELPING HAND and coverage/secondary attacks.

- EXPLOSION sequence: Pokémon below 30% HP + STEAM not active → partner uses AQUATIC that turn to generate STEAM → Coleman detonates next turn. If STEAM already active: immediate detonation.

- Against Rock/Steel: switches to Ground or Fighting coverage. Recognizes structural wall and adapts.

- Against high-frequency player switching: accepts entry burn as passive chip damage — focuses main attacks on the player's second, less-protected Pokémon rather than chasing the Water user.
