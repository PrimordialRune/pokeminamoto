# Story and Setting

Source: Minamoto_Story_v1.1 (primary), GDD v1 "Accenni di Storia", project description. Summary only; see the original for full prose.

## Premise

- Minamoto is a redesign of Pokemon Red/Green (Rosso/Verde) on the Emerald ROM base. Goal: restore and dramatize content lost or softened during original development (darker story, cruder world, villain organisations whose nature was never explicit). Evidence base: leaked prototype data, beta ROM archaeology, Spaceworld 1997 demo.
- Protagonist **Satoshi**, young trainer from **Masara Town**, just licensed. **Dr. Okido** commissions him to catalogue Kanto species in a digital encyclopedia, the **PokeMon Index**. Starts with **Ken** and **Shigeru** (Okido's grandchildren), who then diverge.
- Structure is dramatic irony: first half the player believes Team Rocket is the main villain and Silph Company a neutral institution; second half dismantles this. The key realisation: the player has been working for Silph all along without knowing.

## Tone (foundational commitments)

- Humans and Pokemon are NOT in the warm partnership model of later games. Pokemon are bought and sold in markets; physical discipline (whips, restraints, pain conditioning) is normal; Pokemon are tools/assets/resources first.
- The game never editorialises; it makes this ambient and lets discomfort accumulate.
- Themes: corporate complicity, wartime exploitation, ethics of scientific creation, ambiguous heroism.
- Retro presentation (pixel art, bold colours) with a darker narrative register.

## The World

- **The Pokemon War** ended 10-20 years before the game; within living memory. Pokemon were used as weapons and labour. Visible scars: damaged unrepaired infrastructure, buildings in small towns repurposed from wartime military/industrial use. The Pokemon Tower in Lavender Town is tied to the war dead; Channelers grieve something specific and recent.
- **Lt. Surge** fought and won in the war and is uncomfortable about what winning required. His gym dialogue establishes the war as exploitation rather than heroism.
- Post-war society: Pokemon Markets sit alongside ordinary commerce; species priced by rarity, combat capability, utility. Rough trainers are coded as pragmatists, not villains.

## Factions

- **Team Rocket**: founded by Madame Boss; in the war controlled Kanto's fossil-fuel infrastructure (mining with enslaved Pokemon). Name is a dark phonetic mirror of "Pocket". After the war became a peacetime criminal org, professionalised by Giovanni (uniform, hierarchy). Activities: fossil/rare Pokemon extraction sold to Silph, protection rackets in major cities, running cave mines. Works a fossil site in Mt. Moon.
- **Silph Company**: public face = tech backbone of Kanto (Pokeballs, maintains Bill's PC network, funds research; HQ in Saffron City, largest private building). Hidden program under Blaine, three arms that are really sequential stages toward Mew: (1) fossil revival (from Fuji), (2) Pokemon augmentation via human cellular integration (Blaine), (3) applied Bill digitalization tech.
- **Trade-evolution lineage** (Machamp, Gengar, Alakazam, Golem, ...) is lore-explained as products of Blaine's augmentation experiments; these do not exist in the wild, only in Silph-affiliated hands.
- **Research quests**: scientists across Kanto pay well for specific trade-evolution-line Pokemon (Graveler, Machoke, Kadabra, ...). The player later finds their own name in Silph's specimen acquisition records.

## Plot structure (acts)

1. **Act One - Masara Town to Cerulean City.** Licence, starter, Index commission; rivals depart. Mt. Moon is an active Rocket excavation; player recovers a fossil. At Cerulean, Bill's cottage fusion sidequest (rewarded with SS Anne access).
2. **Act Two - Vermilion to Lavender.** Surge's gym gives war context. Research quest system begins. Lavender: Fuji's house ransacked, notes stolen, Fuji held by Rocket then recovered. Pokemon Tower/war dead. Safari Zone owner tense about his fossil.
3. **Act Three - Rocket Hideout (Celadon).** Hideout under the Celadon Game Corner is the structural pivot. Records show Rocket leadership is at Silph; procurement docs confirm the Safari fossil theft and its destination (Silph labs, Saffron).
4. **Act Four - Silph Company (Saffron).** Floor-by-floor revelation: odd lab, wrong-behaving scientist, Fuji's notes, augmented Pokemon with Silph security, player's name in acquisition records. Giovanni fight on a meeting floor, he stands aside. Blaine on upper floors; defeating him collapses the Mew suppression system, Mew escapes off-screen. Blaine disappears.
5. **Act Five - The League.** Gym badges still matter. Four Heavenly Kings and the League are the apex; winning closes the trainer story, not the plot.
6. **Act Six - Safari Zone (post-League).** Blaine has a field lab in the Safari Zone excavation site, searching for something older than Mew. Deeper Safari areas open. Second fight; he retreats into non-accessible wilderness.
7. **Epilogue - Cerulean Cave.** Opens after the League. Player meets Mew; it is catchable; the game does not instruct the player. This is the true ending.

## Sequel hooks (Gold/Silver)

- Giovanni's disappearance.
- Blaine's excavation findings and the organisation forming around them: origin of ゲルゲ団 (as spelled in the source).
- Mew living in Cerulean Cave, now known to enough people that knowledge will spread.

## Contradictions to note

- GDD abstract describes Ken and Shigeru as nephews ("nipoti") of Dr. Okido; the Story doc calls them his grandchildren. (Italian "nipoti" can mean either.)
- Story doc says Giovanni is not a Gym Leader and Blaine/Katsura hold Cinnabar; arena doc does not cover those gyms.
